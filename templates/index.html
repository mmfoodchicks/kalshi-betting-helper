🏦 Futures — parking money

Long-dated markets our season simulators actually model — World Series, pennants, divisions, playoff berths, season win totals. Every row is our number against the market's, so unlike the all-futures board these can carry a genuinely positive expected return. The model is blended with the price at the weight that sport has earned on graded results, so a league whose model we've measured as no better than the market shows no edge by construction.

Every long-dated Kalshi contract, ranked by what it actually pays per year. A 96¢ contract settling in 2030 returns 4% — over three and a half years, which is worse than a bank. The same 96¢ settling in 60 days is 4% in two months. Same price, same risk per dollar, completely different instrument — so the ranking is annualized.

Read this once. These are not savings accounts. You are buying a contract that pays $1 if it comes true and $0 if it doesn't — a 96¢ contract loses the whole stake 4% of the time, and your money is locked up until it settles. At the market's own price the expected value of every row here is slightly negative (Kalshi's fee). The yield is what you're paid for carrying that tail risk — it is not an edge we found.
