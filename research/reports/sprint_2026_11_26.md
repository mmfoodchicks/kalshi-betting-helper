## C. Findings

### C1. Drive model vs deep engine (Fable; `research/sprint_2026_11_26/engines/report.md`)

- **Mechanism.** The drive model is this week's Sleeper projection. The deep engine's level is Kalshi's
  win-total pace plus half of Sleeper's weekly offence ratio. Home field is the same size in both (equal
  teams 0.5455 / 0.5402, simulated). The card's drive line is calibrated (t = 0.8 sharpens 62.0% raw to
  64.9%) and the deep line is raw. The GB @ LAR gap (64.9% vs 52%) is about 3.4 points of mean margin
  from the level source plus about 3 points of probability from the calibrator.
- **Variance.** Neither engine is wrong. Margin SD is 13.05 (drive) and 13.34 (deep) against a real
  conditional 13.3.
- **The live record.** It compared different games before. On the 57 games both graded (weeks 9-12),
  isolated by differencing the data branch's 72 committed versions:

  | | log loss | Brier |
  |---|---|---|
  | drive | 0.5601 | 0.1901 |
  | deep | 0.5329 | 0.1775 |
  | DraftKings | 0.5243 | 0.1739 |
  | Kalshi | 0.5160 | 0.1702 |

  - Drive minus deep is +0.027 +/- 0.045 over 4 weekly clusters, with the deep engine better in 3 of 4
    weeks. That is not decisive.
  - `accuracy_gaps.md` section 0's "deep worse than drive live" compared different games and is withdrawn
    (corrected in place).
- **Card coherence.** The headline blends the two models. The expected score, ladders, props and the
  same-game parlay are the drive model alone, about 4 points apart on that card. That is noted, not
  changed.
- **Registered deep-weight read.** It triggers at 200 graded `nfl_deep` rows (168 now, about Nov 30 -
  Dec 1). Before it reads, the owner should settle three points in writing:
  1. "200 games" is 200 rows, which is 100 games.
  2. The rule has no branch for "blend ahead on its point estimate, deep not ahead by 2 SE", which is
     exactly where the interim record sits.
  3. Its quoted SE (0.008) is too small. Expect 0.010-0.035.

### C2. Matchups calibration (Fable; `research/sprint_2026_11_26/calibration/report.md`)

- **The data.** NFL rows for weeks 6-11 were rebuilt from the frozen props logs: 20,859 rungs over 82
  games. They reproduce the week-6 report and the live rushing gap. The NBA has aggregates only.
- **NFL is overconfident; Kalshi is not.** Calibration slope (1 is perfect, below 1 is overconfident):

  | | model | Kalshi |
  |---|---|---|
  | all stats | 0.854 +/- 0.029 | 0.978 +/- 0.031 |
  | rushing yards | 0.703 +/- 0.054 | 0.970 |

- **NBA is calibrated but uninformed.** Its slope is about 0.99, yet it loses all five stats: it lacks
  information, not calibration.
- **Why "+EV" loses** (12,144 NFL fee-in +EV bets):

  | step | ROI |
  |---|---|
  | the model's claim | +10.4% |
  | bought at the mid | +2.2% [-1.4, +5.8] |
  | after the fee | -1.2% |
  | after an assumed 2c half-spread | -4.9% |

  - At the real ask, live: NFL -1.7% +/- 1.8, NBA -4.9% +/- 1.1.
  - Only about 5% of a model-market disagreement is real (w = 0.05 +/- 0.08).
  - Realised edge is 12-23% of the claimed edge in every disagreement bucket, so no size filter
    rescues it.
- **Recommendations** (acceptance criteria read only week 13+):
  1. Where a stat is market-better, headline Kalshi's mid and show the engine as "engine says". This is
     display only; board probabilities and record rows stay byte-identical.
  2. Add "blend minus Kalshi" to the blend report. Today's NFL reading is +0.0011 +/- 0.0009 (worse),
     with 8% weight on the engine.
  3. Re-register the line-dependent rushing spread against a rung-level gate. It is a display-accuracy
     fix and does not un-mute rushing (Kalshi still leads by 0.034 +/- 0.009).
  4. Let the live Platt gate read rushing, which is about two weeks away.
  5. Export NBA rows so the NBA can be audited.

### C3. Football conservation (Opus; `research/sprint_2026_11_26/conservation/report.md`)

- **The production path keeps every budget it deals** (`nfl_dfs_engine`). No touchdown is lost or created
  on the engine's own boxes. Every DraftKings score sits on the 0.02 lattice, and on it the lineup buckets
  do not depend on summation order. Off the lattice, six 0.005-grid scores land in different buckets by
  order, which is why the guards keep lattice data.
- **Three latent defects, fixed** (commit d37d3e6):
  - A: the allocator lost touchdowns where a man had room but no weight, and reported no surplus.
  - B: its fixed 12 rounds dealt at most 12 overflows.
  - C: a two-quarterback team could hand one of them an impossible line.
- **Why no board read differently.** A and B could not fire because every caller floors the weight. C
  needed two projected quarterbacks on one team.
- **The fix deals draw for draw as the shipped code** on the one-quarterback path (8 engine boxes x 4,000
  worlds) and on 400 floor-weighted allocator inputs. So there is no ENGINE bump, which would have
  rebuilt every board.
- **Open.** Does DraftKings count opponents' return touchdowns in points allowed? The audit found only a
  third-party summary saying it does. Settling it needs DraftKings' own rules page.

### C4. DFS field and portfolio (Opus; `research/sprint_2026_11_26/dfs/report.md`)

- **Concentration.** On the Thanksgiving MEGA, every one of the money list's shown 8 holds LaPorta,
  Odunze and Emmett Johnson. Pairs of top-20 lineups share 5.4 of 9 players. The 20-entry portfolio
  spreads more but still holds:

  | player | portfolio | field |
  |---|---|---|
  | Odunze | 65% | 29.9% |
  | E. Johnson | 60% | 40.5% |
  | LaPorta | 55% | 22.4% |

  Summing the 20 entries' probabilities would overstate coverage 1.26-1.55x. The cover never does.
- **Why Javonte + Burden beat Gibbs + Raymond.** Both lineups project 134.4. A wins every printed
  column in all three contests (MEGA top 1%: 6.33% vs 4.78%):
  - Gibbs is the steadiest of the four: p90 is 1.49x his mean, against 1.81-2.06x for the others.
  - Pair B's summed p90 is 8.0 points lower.
  - Gibbs is the least-often optimal per $1k.
  - B also downgrades the Chicago stack to its third receiver.

  Ownership is not the reason: B is the less-owned pair.
- **The field.** Week 6 reproduces the known lead: `opt_pct` 6.27 vs `field_pct` 8.28 top-40 MAE.
  The showdown field gives quarterback captains 7-12 points too much. No real field is committed for
  any current board.
- **In-sample figures.** Every list and portfolio figure is ranked on the same worlds it is printed
  from. The classic portfolio is not labelled in-sample.
- **Ranked improvements** (each with its acceptance rule in the report):
  1. Export picked boards.
  2. Log all 20 portfolio entries and check realised coverage against served.
  3. Stamp and measure the classic portfolio's in-sample optimism.
  4. Show held-out list figures.
  5. Add an exposure block.
  6. Test an exposure-capped cover on held-out worlds.
